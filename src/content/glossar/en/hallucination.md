---
title: 'Hallucination'
description: 'A fluent, plausible-sounding statement by an AI model that is not true, such as a made-up quotation or a wrong date.'
translationKey: halluzination
---

A hallucination is a statement by a [language model](/en/glossary/language-model) that sounds confident and fluent but is not true: a made-up book, a wrong date of birth, a source that never existed. The model doesn’t look up facts; it computes a continuation that fits the text so far. If there is no strong learned pattern for a question, an answer still comes out, because the model is supposed to deliver a next text piece at every position. And because tests often rate a guessed answer better than an “I don’t know,” guessing is rewarded on top. Individual facts that appeared rarely in training are especially vulnerable.

**An example:** In 2023, lawyers in New York submitted six court decisions that ChatGPT had generated. None of them existed, and when asked, ChatGPT had even confirmed one of them as real.

**Not to be confused with deception:** The model has no intention of hiding anything. A hallucination is a plausible continuation without backing in learned facts, not an attempt to mislead. Nor is it merely an error in the [training data](/en/glossary/training-data): it also happens when all the data is correct.

**Where you'll come across it:** In notes below chatbot windows that advise you to check important information, in reports about made-up sources or court rulings, and in comparisons that measure how often models get factual questions wrong.

Introduced in [What an AI Model Actually Is](/en/lessons/what-an-ai-model-actually-is).
