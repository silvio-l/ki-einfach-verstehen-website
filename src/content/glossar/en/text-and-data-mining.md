Text and data mining (TDM) means the automated analysis of digital works to obtain information, above all about patterns, trends and connections. In German copyright law, § 44b of the Copyright Act (UrhG) sets the conditions for copies. The exception is no blanket permission for everything one can do with a text.

The exception permits copies (legal term: reproductions) of works that are lawfully accessible, that is, available by a route the law permits. Public availability on its own says nothing reliable about this. Anyone who holds the rights to a work and does not want it used for this can set a [rights reservation](/en/glossary/rights-reservation). Under the wording, such a reservation for works available online takes effect only if it is machine-readable. For research there is a separate rule in § 60d UrhG.

**An example:** A program reads thousands of news articles and counts which words appear together. To read them, it makes copies of the articles. The result, a statistic about word pairs, no longer contains any article. The question is whether the copies were permitted beforehand.

**Not to be confused with training a [model](/en/glossary/model):** TDM describes the analysis of the texts; training is a step that turns them into [parameters](/en/glossary/parameters). Whether the statutory exception also covers training is open and is currently being decided in court.

**Where you'll come across it:** In terms of use and reservations on websites, in statutes, and in reports on lawsuits against AI providers.
