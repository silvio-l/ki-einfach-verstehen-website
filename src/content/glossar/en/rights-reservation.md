A rights reservation is a rights holder’s declaration that their work may not be used for [text and data mining](/en/glossary/text-and-data-mining). Under § 44b(3) UrhG, such a reservation for works available online takes effect only if it is in machine-readable form. A sentence meant only for people, such as one in an imprint, does not meet that requirement by the wording.

Machine-readable means that a program can find and understand the notice. One format for this is the [robots.txt](/en/glossary/robots-txt), which gives a crawler (a program that automatically retrieves web pages) instructions for a website. A reservation takes effect for future uses. What has already been collected is not expressly covered by the wording; how already collected copies are to be treated is open.

**An example:** An operator writes “No use for AI training” in the imprint. A person understands that. A collecting program reads only the machine-readable notice, and without it the sentence has no effect for the program.

**Not to be confused with a license:** A license permits a use, a reservation prohibits a particular one. Whoever sets no reservation does not thereby permit every use; the exception for text and data mining remains a separate statutory basis.

**Where you'll come across it:** In robots.txt files and website settings, in provider pages that describe crawlers, and in court press releases on AI cases.

Introduced in “[Training Data: Copyright and Privacy](/en/lessons/training-data-copyright-and-privacy)” in the section on the reservation only a computer can read. Not legal advice: whether a particular reservation takes effect must be checked case by case.
