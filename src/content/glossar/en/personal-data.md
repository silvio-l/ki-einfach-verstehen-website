Personal data is all information that relates to an identified or identifiable person. It includes names, phone numbers, email addresses, photos of people, and details that make a person recognizable together with other details. The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) applies to it as soon as it is processed, that is, also when texts are collected for [training](/en/glossary/training-data).

The GDPR requires a legal basis for any handling of such data, including storing it. For training with texts from the web, a balancing of interests may come into consideration: a legitimate interest, for example of the provider, is weighed against the rights of the person concerned. If processing rests on that balancing, the person can object under Art. 21(1) GDPR, on grounds relating to their particular situation. Whether data already processed is covered by such an objection is not generally settled.

**An example:** A forum post gives a name and a phone number. The text therefore contains personal data, even if the rest of the post is only about gardening. A collecting program captures the whole post, so the details end up in the dataset as well.

**Not to be confused with a work:** That a text is protected by copyright says nothing about whether it contains personal data. Both questions can apply at once.

**Where you'll come across it:** In privacy notices from services, in objection forms, and in reports on whether models can reproduce personal details from their training data.
