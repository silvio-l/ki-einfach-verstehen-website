---
title: 'Lateral reading'
description: 'You check a claim by leaving the page and looking outside it: who makes the claim, and what do other sources say?'
translationKey: seitwaertslesen
---

**The basic idea:** Lateral reading (German: „Seitwärtslesen“) is a way of checking in which you examine a claim outside the page: you open new tabs, search for who stands behind a page, and compare the claim with other sources.

**An example:** A website gives a statistic without a source and looks serious. Someone reading laterally leaves the page after a quick look and searches for the operator’s name and the figure on other sites. If the figure only appears on pages that cite each other, he has not checked it independently, because they all rely only on each other.

**Where you meet the term:** With chatbots that name sources. You open the named source outside the chat instead of reading on in the bot’s answer. Fact-checkers work this way. The same applies to images and quotes: you look for the first place where they appeared and read the context there. A study by [Wineburg and McGrew (2019)](https://purl.stanford.edu/yk133ht8603) shows how professional fact-checkers assess information online: they leave the page and search outside for information about its origin. The link leads to the 2018 preprint of the study, that is, the version before publication; the study was published in 2019. The study does not show that laypeople can learn this approach in a short time.
